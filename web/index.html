    What would a fly do?

    Show it a picture. The picture moves in front of the fly; each change in brightness drives the eye columns that see it, and the connectome carries that to the fly's visual feature detectors and the neurons that command its body.

      What is real here

      The wiring is real. The brain is the FlyWire connectome (v783): 138,639 neurons of an adult female Drosophila and 15,091,983 connections, each weighted by its synapse count, excitatory or inhibitory.

      The model is published. Neurons follow the leaky integrate-and-fire model of Shiu et al. (Nature, 2024), with their parameters, run here on your GPU with WebGPU in 0.1 ms steps. Every verdict is computed live; nothing is scripted.

      The split of work. A food recognizer matches each photo against about 1,400 dishes; each dish's taste profile (sweet, bitter, salty, watery) is computed from USDA nutrient data: sugars, sodium, water, caffeine and theobromine per 100 g, plus the share of bitter ingredients such as arugula or grapefruit. The fly only ever receives taste: sugar, bitter, water and Ir94e (salt) taste neurons fire at up to 150 Hz. It "eats" when MN9, the proboscis motor neuron, fires 4 spikes within 50 ms. Preferences like bitterness vetoing sugar come from the connectome.

      "Show it a picture" (experimental). The picture moves in front of the fly; its change in brightness, computed per eye column outside the connectome, drives about 31,000 medulla and lobula-plate neurons, and the connectome carries that to visual projection neurons and descending "command" neurons. Their links to behaviour (escape, steering, walking) come from experiments on real flies and were not validated in this model.

      What is not modeled: smell, learning, neuromodulation, hunger hormones, the body. The fly-eye view is decoration and never affects the pick. This is a joke app with a real core, not a fly's mind.
